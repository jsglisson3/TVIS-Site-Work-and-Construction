## 2. Facebook/Instagram ads → landing page

### What contractors link to
- A PipelineOn audit of 1,222 active US home-service Facebook advertisers found **15.7% used Meta Lead Forms and only 2.9% used a dedicated landing page**. The rest pointed elsewhere (homepage, Messenger or call). This comes from a vendor/agency blog, June 2026. https://pipelineon.com/blog/facebook-lead-form-vs-landing-page-contractors/
- The same source's rule of thumb is Lead Forms for jobs under $1k, a landing page for jobs over $5k, and testing both for $1k–$5k. It says to judge on **cost per booked job, not cost per lead**. It estimates Lead Form leads close at about 5–15% and landing-page leads at about 15–30%. These are the article's own estimates with no cited data. Same URL.
- In an independent A/B test, Jon Loomer ran identical ads to an Instant Form and to a website form. The results: CPL $2.04 vs $3.77, the same "quality" rate (29%), and website leads more deliverable (92% vs 84%). This was a lead magnet, not a contractor offer, so it may not transfer to sitework. https://www.jonloomer.com/testing-quality-leads/
- **Recommendation for TVIS.** Most TVIS jobs fall in the $1k–$5k band, and photos are central to quoting. Point ads at a **dedicated single-service landing page** with a photo-upload estimate form. Optionally run a Meta Instant Form ad set in parallel as a test. Instant Forms can't take photos, so follow up by text and ask for photos.

### What high-converting home-service landing pages contain
- From an agency blog (Stackmatix): headline that matches the ad, sticky click-to-call, trust signals above the fold (rating, review count, years, licensed/insured), short form (name, phone, service, timing), "Get a Free Estimate" instead of "Submit", a form near the top and again at the bottom, 3–5 reviews that mention the specific service, real crew/truck/job photos, tap targets of 48px or more, and images under 100KB. Its numbers ("landing pages convert 3–5x homepages", "60–70% mobile") are unsourced agency claims. https://www.stackmatix.com/blog/home-services-landing-pages-that-convert
- **Unbounce benchmark (2024 report, vendor data):** home improvement median landing-page conversion was **2.6%** vs 3.2% across all industries, with the top quartile at 19.5%. Pages mixing click, form and call CTAs converted at 4%. General home-services pages did best at about 300 words. https://unbounce.com/industry/home-improvement/
- **Meta's own guide** (Meta Consumer Growth Consulting with Branding Brand, 500 destination pages analysed, Apr 2022) says to prioritise **speed, answers to common questions, and mobile-friendly interfaces**. https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/meta-publishes-new-guide-on-landing-page-best-practices/622743/
- **Speed:** Google found 53% of mobile visits are abandoned if a page takes longer than 3 seconds to load. This is old (2016) but still the most cited first-party figure. https://www.marketingdive.com/news/google-53-of-mobile-users-abandon-sites-that-take-over-3-seconds-to-load/

### Single-service landing page template (recommended)
1. **Slim header:** logo plus a tap-to-call button (`tel:`). No full nav menu, or nav collapsed so it doesn't compete with the form. Add a footer link to the main site for trust.
2. **Hero:** an H1 that repeats the ad's promise (e.g. ad "Driveway full of ruts?" → H1 "Gravel Driveway Repair in Chattanooga — Ruts & Potholes Fixed"). One real before/after photo, 3 trust bullets (local, insured, free estimates, fast response), and a primary CTA "Get My Free Estimate", which scrolls to the form or opens step 1.
3. **Estimate form** near the top (see section 3), plus a secondary "Call / Text" button.
4. **Before/after gallery** of 3–6 real jobs for this service, with captions naming the town.
5. **"How it works":** 3 steps (send photos → quote → scheduled).
6. **3–5 Google reviews** about this service, with reviewer first name and town.
7. **Short FAQ** of 4–6 questions on price drivers, timeline, gravel type, culvert sizing, permits/811. This covers Meta's "answers to common questions" point.
8. **Service area line** (towns list) and **repeat form/CTA** at the bottom.
9. **Sticky bottom bar on mobile:** Call | Text | Free Estimate.
10. **Tech:** static HTML, inline critical CSS, one hero image of about 100KB in WebP/AVIF with width/height set, no sliders, no chat widgets, and the Meta Pixel loaded `async`. Fire a `Lead` event on the thank-you page. Keep UTMs (see section 3).
- Build one page per advertised service: `/lp/gravel-driveway/`, `/lp/drainage/`, `/lp/shed-pad/`. Mark them `noindex` if they duplicate the SEO service pages. Otherwise they compete with the SEO pages and look like thin duplicates.

## 3. Estimate form

### Best practices
- **Fields (keep about 5–7 visible):** Name*, Phone*, Service (tap buttons/select)*, Address or town / ZIP*, Short description, Photos (optional), Email (optional), Preferred contact (call/text). Stackmatix recommends name + phone + service dropdown + timing, with optional fields clearly marked. https://www.stackmatix.com/blog/home-services-landing-pages-that-convert
- **Required vs optional:** clearly distinguish them, put labels above fields, show errors inline next to the field, and never clear completed fields on error. A Google Research / University of Basel study found that applying these guidelines significantly improved completion time, first-try success and satisfaction. https://research.google/blog/simple-is-better-making-your-web-forms-easy-to-use-pays-off/
- **Phone vs email:** make phone required and email optional. Contractors call or text back, and a 5-minute response is advised (PipelineOn, agency). https://pipelineon.com/blog/facebook-lead-form-vs-landing-page-contractors/
- **Multi-step vs single:** a multi-step form works well here. Step 1 is tap-a-service (easy, no typing), step 2 is photos + description, step 3 is name/phone/address. Venture Harbour reports large lifts, but the claims are anecdotal with no sample sizes, and the publisher sells a multi-step form tool. Its practical tips are still sound: easy question first, 1–2 questions per step, a progress bar, contact details last. https://ventureharbour.com/multi-step-lead-forms-get-300-conversions/ Build it as **one `<form>` with JS-toggled fieldsets**, so it still submits as a single POST and degrades gracefully without JS.
- **Photo upload on mobile:**
  - `<input type="file" name="photos" accept="image/*" multiple>`. On most phones this offers both Camera and Photo Library.
  - **Do not add `capture`** if you want gallery access. `capture="environment"` asks for the rear camera, and the browser decides whether the gallery is offered. https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/HTML/Element/Input/file
  - `accept` is only a hint, so validate on the server side. Same MDN URL.
  - Requires `method="POST" enctype="multipart/form-data"`. https://help.formspree.io/articles/building-your-form/file-uploads.md
  - Phone photos are often 3–8MB. Consider client-side resizing to about 1600px JPEG with `<canvas>` before upload. This saves storage and avoids upload timeouts. Formspree times out requests over 30s, which can bite on slow rural connections. https://help.formspree.io/hc/en-us/articles/115008380088
  - Label it "Add photos (optional, up to 5) — helps us quote faster", and show thumbnails/count after selection.
- **Spam without CAPTCHA:**
  - **Honeypot:** a hidden text field that bots fill. Formspree's is `_gotcha` (all plans), and submissions with it filled are silently dropped. https://help.formspree.io/articles/building-your-form/honeypot-spam-filtering.md
  - **Time check:** set a hidden `form_started` timestamp on load, and have JS block or flag submits made in under 3 seconds. This is a common pattern, not a vendor feature. Pair it with the service's server-side filter.
  - Rely on the provider's built-in filter (Formspree "basic"; Basin SpamCheck AI on all plans, per a review site). https://formspree.io/plans , https://splitforms.com/reviews/basin
- **UTM capture:** on load, read `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`, `utm_content` and `fbclid` from `location.search`. Store them in `sessionStorage`, wrapped in try/catch, and write them into hidden inputs, plus `landing_page` and `referrer`. The notification email then shows which ad produced the lead.
- **Thank-you page:** redirect to `/thanks/` (or `/thanks/?service=driveway`). It should confirm what happens next ("We'll call or text within X hours"), show the phone number, include a "Send more photos by text" link (`sms:`), and fire the Meta Pixel `Lead` event there. Submitting via fetch/AJAX and then `location.href='/thanks/'` works on any plan.

## 4. Local SEO for a service-area business

- **Google Business Profile as a SAB**
  - Remove the street address if customers aren't served there.
  - Set up to **20 service areas** by city, ZIP or region. Radius areas aren't allowed.
  - Keep the overall area within about **2 hours' drive** of the base.
  - https://support.google.com/business/answer/9157481?hl=en
  - Primary category is something like "Excavating contractor". Add secondary categories (e.g. "Drainage service", "Concrete contractor", "Grading contractor") only where they're true. Category names are suggestions; check the list in GBP.
  - Fill every field and add photos and videos. "Businesses with complete and accurate info are more likely to show up." Ranking is driven by **relevance, distance, prominence**. Reply to reviews. https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091?hl=en
  - Post job photos regularly, captioned with the town.
- **NAP consistency:** use one exact business name, phone number and URL everywhere: site footer, GBP, Facebook, Instagram, BBB, Nextdoor, Angi/HomeAdvisor and Yelp. With a hidden address, list the city/state only. BrightLocal guidance on citations: https://brightlocal.com/learn/local-citations/managing-local-citations/optimizing-local-citations/
- **Schema** (JSON-LD on the homepage and contact page)
  - Use the most specific `LocalBusiness` subtype Google supports. Google requires `name` and `address`, and recommends `telephone`, `url`, `geo`, `openingHoursSpecification` and `priceRange`. https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/local-business?hl=en
  - Use `HomeAndConstructionBusiness`, or the subtype `GeneralContractor`.
  - For a SAB, include `address` with only `addressLocality: "Chattanooga"`, `addressRegion: "TN"` and `postalCode`, with no street. This is common practice, since Google's doc doesn't address SABs.
  - Add `areaServed` as an array of `City` objects (Hixson, Ooltewah, Ringgold GA…).
  - Add `hasOfferCatalog` / `Service` items for each service, and `sameAs` linking the Facebook, Instagram and GBP URLs.
  - Validate with the Rich Results Test (https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/establish-business-details?authuser=1).
  - Don't mark up your own `aggregateRating`. Google lists review markup as only for sites reviewing *other* businesses (same local-business doc).
- **Service pages vs city pages**
  - Build **one strong page per service** (8 pages, matching section 1) and one "Service Area" page listing towns, with real jobs per area.
  - Don't spin "/gravel-driveway-hixson/", "/gravel-driveway-ooltewah/"… with swapped town names. Google's spam policy names "multiple domain names or pages targeted at specific regions or cities that funnel users to one page" as doorway abuse. https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies
  - Add a city page only when it has unique content: local projects with photos, specific soil/terrain notes (e.g. Signal Mountain rock, Ringgold GA permitting), and reviews from that town.
- **Titles/meta:** one unique `<title>` per page with service + "Chattanooga, TN" + brand (patterns in section 1), and a meta description of about 150 chars with a CTA ("Free estimates — send photos").
- **Reviews:** after every job, text the GBP review link (`g.page/r/.../review`) and reply to every review (support.google.com/business/answer/7091 above). Show reviews on the site as quoted text or a static embed. Avoid heavy third-party widgets for speed.
- **Images/alt text:**
  - Use real job photos with descriptive filenames (`gravel-driveway-repair-hixson.webp`, not `IMG_0023.jpg`).
  - Write alt text that describes what's shown, with keywords used naturally and no stuffing.
  - Place images near related text.
  - https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/114016
- **Sitemap:** commit `sitemap.xml` (and `robots.txt` pointing to it) to the repo and submit it in Search Console. Exclude or `noindex` the ad landing pages and `/thanks/`. Images can go into the sitemap too (same Google image doc).
- **Core Web Vitals:** aim for "good" at the 75th percentile, meaning **LCP ≤ 2.5s, INP ≤ 200ms, CLS ≤ 0.1**. https://web.dev/articles/vitals (summarised at https://specification.website/spec/performance/core-web-vitals.md)
  - On a static GitHub Pages site, the main risks are oversized hero photos, web fonts, and third-party scripts such as the pixel, chat and review widgets.
  - Use WebP/AVIF with `srcset` and `width`/`height`, `loading="lazy"` below the fold, `fetchpriority="high"` on the hero, system fonts or one self-hosted font, and defer all JS.
- **GitHub Pages specifics:** use a custom domain with HTTPS enforced, and a 404 page. Set Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster up on the custom domain.
