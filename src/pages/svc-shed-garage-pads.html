Pads for Sheds & Garages

A level, well-drained pad, gravel or concrete, keeps a shed door from sticking and a garage slab from cracking. This is how a TVIS pad goes in, from a sloped corner of the yard to a building that sits square.

How a TVIS pad goes in

  1. {{photo slot="pads-step-1" label="cleared, staked area" alt="Pad area laid out with wood forms in an open yard" sizes="(min-width: 900px) 25vw, 50vw"}}

    Cleared site

    Brush, roots and topsoil come out so the pad sits on firm ground.

  2. {{photo slot="pads-step-2" label="graded dirt pad" alt="Graded dirt pad inside the forms" sizes="(min-width: 900px) 25vw, 50vw"}}

    Graded pad

    Cut and filled level, with the edges sloped so water runs away.

  3. {{photo slot="pads-step-3" label="compacted gravel base" alt="Concrete slab poured on the finished pad" sizes="(min-width: 900px) 25vw, 50vw"}}

    Compacted gravel base

    Stone placed in layers and compacted, so it stays flat.

  4. {{photo slot="pads-step-4" label="finished shed on the pad" alt="Finished building standing on the pad" sizes="(min-width: 900px) 25vw, 50vw"}}

    Finished building

    Your shed, garage or metal building goes up on a solid base.
